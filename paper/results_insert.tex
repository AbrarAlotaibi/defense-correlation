\subsection{Measuring failure correlation between stacked defenses}
\label{sec:corr-measure}

Section~\ref{sec:composition} argued that layered defenses compound only when their failures
are independent. We now test that premise directly. For each defense $d$ and behaviour $i$
we record a binary breach $b_d(i)\in\{0,1\}$ under a gold judge, and for each pair report
the marginals $\hat p_1,\hat p_2$, the joint
$\mathrm{ASR}_{d_1d_2}=\tfrac1n\sum_i b_{d_1}(i)b_{d_2}(i)$, the excess over independence
$\Delta=\mathrm{ASR}_{d_1d_2}-\hat p_1\hat p_2$, and the failure correlation $\phi$, which is
the $\rho$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:joint}.

\paragraph{Setup.}
$n=100$ JailbreakBench behaviours with the matched benign set. Seven defenses span the
dependency rows of Table~\ref{tab:dependency}: a windowed perplexity filter and a surface
token-anomaly filter (token surface); Llama~Guard~3~8B (semantic classification); a
refusal-priming prefix (first-token distribution); SmoothLLM, $q{=}4$, $10\%$ character
perturbation (perturbation stability); and linear probes on hidden states at layers 16 and 8
(internal representations). Every thresholded filter has its threshold set so that it
\emph{blocks} $1\%$ of the benign prompts (a $1\%$ filter false-positive rate); the realised
block rate is $0.010$ for all four thresholded filters. This is a different quantity from the
system-level false-refusal rate used in H3, which also counts prompts the base model declines
of its own accord (\S\ref{sec:h3-note}). The in-loop attack signal is the target logprob of the affirmative
prefix; the StrongREJECT autograder scores breach \emph{post hoc} and never participates in
the search.

\paragraph{The attack class determines what can be measured.}
\label{par:attack-class}
We ran two adaptive adversaries. GCG optimises an adversarial suffix of arbitrary tokens;
AutoDAN performs a sentence-level genetic search over fluent prompts. Their prompts differ
sharply in surface statistics, and the perplexity filter is calibrated at a windowed-NLL
threshold of $4.85$:

Under GCG the perplexity filter blocks $100/100$ attack prompts and its residual ASR is
exactly $0$, so every pair containing it has an \emph{undefined} $\phi$. Under AutoDAN it
blocks $0/100$ and its residual ASR is $0.66$. \textbf{The filter's apparent invulnerability
is a property of the attack class, not of the defense.} This is the central methodological
caution of this section: a defense evaluated only against high-perplexity suffix attacks
will be scored as near-perfect and will contribute a spurious zero to any composition
estimate. All results below therefore use the fluent adversary, which admits every defense
into the measurement.

\paragraph{H1: same-row defenses fail together. Supported.}
Both within-row pairs show strong positive correlation. Two probes reading the same residual
stream at different depths reach $\phi=0.75$ ($[0.62,0.88]$), with a joint residual of $0.58$
against an independence prediction of $0.41$. The pre-registered token-surface pair, which
could not be evaluated under GCG because the perplexity filter admitted nothing, is
measurable under the fluent adversary and gives $\phi=0.35$ ($[0.18,0.50]$, $q<0.001$).

The support is uneven, and we state that rather than average over it. Of the two same-row
pairs, only the probe pair survives the difficulty-stratified test of \S\ref{sec:confound}
(common odds ratio $159$, $q<0.001$); the pre-registered token-surface pair does not (common
odds ratio $1.39$, $p=0.91$), so its $\phi=0.35$ is consistent with a shared difficulty
gradient rather than a shared mechanism. H1 is therefore supported on the pair whose members
share an input representation, and the confirmatory pair supports it only before
stratification. Since the surviving pair is also the exploratory one added by amendment
(\S\ref{sec:limits-corr}), the mechanism-specific reading of H1 rests on a single post-hoc
pair and should be treated as suggestive rather than confirmed.

\paragraph{H2: cross-row defenses are \emph{not} independent. Rejected.}
Every one of the thirteen non-degenerate cross-row pairs shows a positive failure
correlation, all significant after multiplicity correction, with $\phi$ ranging from $0.30$
to $0.62$. Defenses that share no mechanism --- a surface statistic, a mid-network linear
probe, a first-token prior, and a randomised-smoothing wrapper --- nonetheless fail on
overlapping inputs. The effect replicates across targets and attack classes: the
probe~$\times$~refusal-prime correlation is $0.21$ on Llama-3.2-3B, $0.26$ on Llama-2-7B and
$0.25$ on Vicuna under GCG, and $0.45$ under the fluent attack.

The practical consequence is immediate: the multiplicative model systematically overstates a
stack's protection. For perplexity~$\times$~probe$_{16}$ the independence prediction is
$0.449$ while the measured joint residual is $0.580$, and the pattern holds for every pair we
measured, with $\Delta>0$ throughout and $\Delta$ up to $0.172$. \textbf{Layered defenses
compound only under independence, and independence does not hold in practice.}

\S\ref{sec:confound} shows that most of this cross-row association is attributable to a
common cause --- variation in how jailbreakable individual behaviours are --- rather than to
mechanism overlap. That distinction matters for interpreting Table~\ref{tab:dependency}, but
it does not soften the composition result: whether layers fail together because they share a
blind spot or because they face a common difficulty gradient, the joint residual exceeds
$\hat p_1\hat p_2$ and the stack under-delivers.

\paragraph{H3: the refusal budget binds. Supported.}
\label{sec:h3-note}
The quantity $f_i$ here is the \emph{system-level} false refusal rate: the fraction of benign
prompts on which the deployed configuration returns a refusal, whether because the filter
blocked the prompt or because the base model declined it unprompted. It is therefore larger
than the $1\%$ filter block rate the thresholds were calibrated to: measured directly, the
undefended model refuses $8$ of the $100$ benign prompts, and every defended configuration
refuses a superset of exactly those eight. Measured $f_i$ are refusal-prime $0.39$,
Llama~Guard $0.26$, SmoothLLM $0.26$, probe$_8$ $0.09$, token-anomaly $0.09$, perplexity
$0.08$ and probe$_{16}$ $0.08$.

Reporting those rates raw overstates what the defenses cost. Netting out the floor --- the
rate at which a configuration refuses a prompt the bare model did \emph{not} refuse --- gives
an attributable burden of $0.337$ for refusal-prime, $0.196$ for Llama~Guard and SmoothLLM,
$0.011$ for probe$_8$ and token-anomaly, and \textbf{$0.000$ for perplexity and
probe$_{16}$}: those two refuse on precisely the floor and nothing else, so their entire
apparent false-refusal cost belongs to the base model. The assembled
stack refuses $0.810$ of benign prompts, against an independence prediction of
$1-\prod_i(1-f_i)=0.766$ and a union bound of $\sum_i f_i=1.25$. The observed rate lies
between the two, close to the independence prediction and comfortably inside the bound.
Refusal budget is therefore exhausted long before inference cost becomes the binding
constraint, exactly as Eq.~\eqref{eq:refusal} predicts: a seven-layer stack that admits only
$1\%$ of adaptive attacks also refuses four in five benign requests, which is not a
deployable operating point.

\paragraph{Intersection versus a direct attack on the stack.}
Failure correlation is estimated by intersecting per-defense breach vectors, but the
quantity a deployer cares about is the residual of the assembled stack under an adversary
that attacks \emph{it}. We measure both. Intersecting the seven per-defense vectors predicts
a joint residual of $0.010$; running the same fluent adversary directly against the
assembled stack yields $0.000$, and the two agree on $99$ of $100$ behaviours. That
agreement does establish that the pairwise analysis above is not an artefact of never having
attacked the real system. It is weak evidence for anything more, because both quantities lie
on the floor, where any method returning near-zero would agree.
The single behaviour that survives the intersection is the same one Llama~Guard fails on;
the direct attack on the assembled stack breaches none. McNemar against the strongest single
layer --- the mid-layer probe, at $\hat p = 0.68$, not one of the near-perfect filters ---
gives $68$ behaviours breached by the probe but held by the stack, $0$ in the reverse
direction ($p = 7\times 10^{-21}$).

To give the comparison resolution we repeated it with Llama~Guard removed, since its residual
of $0.01$ is what pins the intersection to the floor. Over the remaining six defenses the
intersection predicts a substantial residual of $0.190$. A direct fluent attack on that
assembled six-layer stack yields $0.000$, disagreeing with the intersection by the full
$0.190$. The mechanism is joint infeasibility rather than the stack defeating successful
attacks: every attempt was rejected at the input stage, and the search failed to find a prompt
satisfying all six constraints simultaneously on $100$ of $100$ behaviours. Optimising against
each layer separately and intersecting implicitly assumes that a prompt defeating one layer
survives the others when they are present, and the joint feasible region is far smaller than
that assumption allows.

The intersection estimate is therefore not a faithful proxy for an assembled stack in general.
It is conservative for the deployer and optimistic for the attacker, and on the one
configuration where the check can discriminate, the error is as large as the quantity being
estimated. We accordingly report the pairwise analysis as a measurement of correlation between
defenses, which is what it is, rather than as a prediction of assembled-stack residual. The
claim this licenses is bounded by the attack budget: under a search sufficient to produce a
$0.190$ per-layer intersection residual, a direct attack finds no feasible prompt on any
behaviour --- a statement about that search, not a proof that the feasible region is empty.

understate residual risk by more than an order of magnitude on that target as well.

\paragraph{Where the optimiser underperforms a fixed suffix.}
\label{par:inversion}
In the \emph{suffix}-attack runs the optimised attack is occasionally less successful than a
static baseline: on Vicuna the perplexity filter records $0.00$ adaptive against $0.07$ for
the plain prompt, and SmoothLLM $0.02$ against $0.06$ for the transfer suffix; on Llama-2 the
same inversion appears for four defenses. We checked that this is not a labelling error:
adaptive rows carry per-behaviour optimised suffixes, plain rows are byte-identical to the
behaviour text, and transfer rows carry the shared fixed suffix. The mechanism is the one in
\S\ref{par:attack-class} --- every inverted case is a defense that neutralises the
high-perplexity suffix class, so the constrained optimiser is driven into a region where its
output is blocked or destabilised while an unmodified prompt passes unflagged. Taken
individually each inversion is $2$--$6$ breaches at $n=100$ and well within sampling noise;
taken together the direction is systematic.

Two consequences follow. First, the adaptive column reports the optimiser's own output rather
than a best-of-strategies adversary; a deployer should read
$\max(\text{adaptive},\text{static})$, which in the suffix runs raises the perplexity filter
to $0.07$ and SmoothLLM to $0.06$. Second, and more important for everything above,
\textbf{no inversion occurs in either fluent run}: the primary Vicuna results and the Llama-2
replication are free of it, so Table~\ref{tab:dependency}, H1--H3, the stratified analysis and
the intersection comparison are unaffected.

\subsection{Is the correlation just behaviour difficulty?}
\label{sec:confound}

A positive $\phi$ between two defenses does not by itself establish a shared blind spot.
Behaviours differ in how readily they are jailbroken, and if every defense fails on the same
easy behaviours the pairwise correlations follow from that common cause alone. Because this
is the most consequential alternative explanation of the results above, we test it
explicitly rather than leaving it to interpretation.

For each pair $(d_1,d_2)$ we stratify the behaviours by a difficulty score computed from the
\emph{other} defenses only --- the number of the remaining live defenses that were breached
on that behaviour --- and compute the Cochran--Mantel--Haenszel (CMH) common odds ratio,
which measures the $d_1$--$d_2$ association \emph{within} strata of equal difficulty.
Deriving the stratifier from the other defenses keeps it independent of the pair under test.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llcccc}
\toprule

\midrule
probe$_{16}$ $\times$ probe$_{8}$   & same  & 0.75 & 68.0 & $\mathbf{158.7}$ ($p<0.001$) & $\mathbf{<0.001}$ \\
refusal-prime $\times$ SmoothLLM    & cross & 0.56 & 12.3 & $4.34$ ($p=0.023$) & $0.173$ \\
perplexity $\times$ refusal-prime   & cross & 0.58 & 14.5 & $3.95$ ($p=0.063$) & $0.314$ \\
perplexity $\times$ probe$_{8}$     & cross & 0.62 & 19.1 & $3.07$ ($p=0.21$)  & $0.562$ \\
perplexity $\times$ token-anomaly   & same  & 0.35 & 6.0  & $1.39$ ($p=0.91$)  & $0.943$ \\
probe$_{16}$ $\times$ refusal-prime & cross & 0.45 & 7.3  & $0.72$ ($p=0.94$)  & $0.943$ \\
probe$_{8}$ $\times$ SmoothLLM      & cross & 0.48 & 7.8  & $0.91$ ($p=0.84$)  & $0.943$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The pattern is clear and it sharpens the reading of Table~\ref{tab:dependency}. Of fifteen
pairs, only two retain a positive within-stratum association at the nominal level, and
after Benjamini--Hochberg correction across all fifteen only one does ($q$ column): the
same-row probe pair, whose common odds ratio of $159$ is an
order of magnitude above its crude value. Three cross-row pairs have CMH odds ratios at or
below unity, and the remainder are underpowered once the sample is divided into strata of
roughly fifteen behaviours each.

\textbf{Interpretation.} The two same-row pairs behave differently, and the difference is
instructive. The probe pair retains a large within-stratum association, so its members share a
blind spot specific to their mechanism. The pre-registered token-surface pair does not: its
crude odds ratio of $6.0$ falls to $1.39$ within strata ($p=0.91$), which is what a pure
difficulty effect looks like. Same-row membership is therefore not sufficient on its own for
mechanism-specific correlated failure; what the surviving pair has in addition is a shared
\emph{input representation}, two probes reading the same residual stream at different depths.
Cross-row defenses do fail together,
but predominantly because they face a common difficulty gradient rather than because they
share a mechanism. Row membership therefore predicts \emph{mechanism-specific} correlated
failure, which is the substantive content of Table~\ref{tab:dependency}; the residual
cross-row dependence is real but common-cause. Both regimes violate the independence
assumption that multiplicative composition requires, so the practical conclusion of
\S\ref{sec:corr-measure} is unchanged.

\textbf{Two caveats, stated plainly.} The test is deliberately conservative: conditioning on
the outcomes of other defenses removes shared variance that is arguably part of the effect
of interest, so it under-credits genuine mechanism overlap. And with $n=100$ split across
about six strata it is underpowered --- ten of the fifteen pairs return wide intervals, so
``not significant within strata'' should be read as insufficient evidence rather than as
evidence of no effect. A larger behaviour set, or pooling across targets, would sharpen
this analysis, and we flag it as the most valuable extension of this experiment.

\subsection{Replication on a strongly aligned target}
\label{sec:replication}

Vicuna-7B is weakly aligned by construction, so we repeat the experiment on
Llama-2-7b-chat with the \emph{same} fluent adversary and the \emph{same} attack budget,
varying only the target. Following the experiment plan, the replication evaluates the four
defenses the headline pairs require on the first $50$ behaviours.

Two findings replicate cleanly and one does not.

\paragraph{The attack-class result replicates.}
On Llama-2 the perplexity filter admits the fluent attack exactly as it did on Vicuna: its
residual ASR is $0.44$, against $0.00$ for the same defense under GCG on the same target
(\S\ref{par:attack-class}). The adaptive--static gap replicates at the same magnitude
($0.44$ adaptive versus $0.00$ static for the perplexity filter; $0.42$ for the early-layer
probe). The undefended residual is $0.40$, so the fluent adversary is effective against a
refusal-robust target, not only against a weakly aligned one. This is the finding we regard
as established across targets.

\paragraph{The correlation structure is directionally consistent but underpowered.}
Every pair that is measurable on both targets keeps its sign, and the two probes remain the
most strongly associated pair ($\phi=0.25$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.03,0.52]$, versus $0.75$ on
Vicuna), but at $n=50$ with lower marginals the intervals are wide and only
perplexity~$\times$~probe$_{8}$ reaches nominal significance ($\phi=0.35$, $[0.08,0.61]$,
$p=0.015$, $q=0.089$). We therefore report the replication as \emph{consistent in direction
and not independently confirmatory}: it does not contradict the Vicuna result, and it does
not on its own establish it. A full-size replication is the natural next step.

\paragraph{The pre-registered same-row pair remains untestable.}
On Llama-2 the token-anomaly filter admits almost nothing under the fluent attack (residual
ASR $0.02$, one breach in fifty), so perplexity~$\times$~token-anomaly is again degenerate
--- this time because the \emph{second} member of the pair is near-constant rather than the
first. The pre-registered same-row test is thus measurable on the weakly aligned target only.
That a row becomes untestable exactly when one of its members is highly effective is a
structural limitation of correlation-based composition analysis, not a property of these two
filters (\S\ref{sec:limits-corr}).

\subsection{Complementary coverage: what survives a fluent attack}
\label{sec:complementary}
Llama~Guard~3 is the one defense that holds under both adversaries, with residual ASR $0.00$
under GCG and $0.01$ under AutoDAN, at the cost of the second-highest false-refusal rate
($0.26$). The perplexity filter shows the opposite profile: near-perfect against
high-perplexity suffixes and largely ineffective ($0.66$ residual) against fluent prompts.
The two are complementary in \emph{attack-class coverage} rather than in per-input failure,
which is the practical reading of the correlations above: a useful stack pairs layers whose
blind spots lie in different attack classes, not merely in different dependency rows.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits-corr}
\textbf{Post-hoc pair.} The probe$_{16}\times$probe$_{8}$ pair was added after observing that
the pre-registered same-row pair was unmeasurable under GCG; it is reported as exploratory,
and the two instances share an input representation, so it bounds the same-row effect from
the easy side. The pre-registered token-surface pair is confirmatory and is reported above.
\textbf{Attack implementation.} Our fluent adversary is an AutoDAN-\emph{style} sentence-level
genetic search over a fixed pool of framings; the released AutoDAN additionally uses an LLM
for paraphrase mutation, which would likely raise ASR further and can only strengthen the
direction of these findings.
\textbf{Target strength and replication power.} The primary target is weakly aligned, giving
high residual ASRs and well-powered correlations. The Llama-2-7B replication
(\S\ref{sec:replication}) reproduces the attack-class result and the adaptive--static gap,
but at $n=50$ its correlation estimates are directionally consistent rather than
independently confirmatory; the correlation claims therefore rest on the single primary
target, and a full-size replication remains outstanding.
\textbf{Untestable rows.} H1 is evaluated in two of the five rows. The semantic-classification
row admits no within-row test on this data for a substantive reason rather than a practical
one: Llama~Guard's residual ASR is $0.01$, so any second instance in that row would face a
near-constant breach vector and an undefined $\phi$. A row that is highly effective against
the available adversary cannot be assessed for internal correlation, which is itself a
limitation of correlation-based composition analysis.
\textbf{Statistical power and seeds.} Results come from a single attack seed at $n=100$; the
bootstrap intervals quantify sampling over behaviours but not the stochasticity of the
genetic search itself. The stratified analysis of \S\ref{sec:confound} is underpowered by
construction.
\textbf{Probe validity.} The internal-representations defense is a probe we trained
ourselves; its validation AUROC is near-ceiling on a held-out split of the training pools,
which may reflect topical separability between the harmful and benign corpora as much as a
representation of harmfulness. Its operating threshold is calibrated on the
\emph{same} $100$ benign prompts that are later used to report false-refusal rates, so every
threshold in this study is set in-sample, and at $n=100$ a $1\%$ target rate is a single
prompt. We have since measured that margin rather than leaving it unknown. Recalibrating on an
external corpus of $805$ general instructions, disjoint from both the evaluation set and the
probe's training pool, moves the surface filters only slightly and in the permissive
direction, but moves probe$_{16}$ so far that at its external threshold it would refuse $26\%$
of the held-out benign set instead of $1\%$. The evaluation benign prompts are matched
counterparts to the harmful behaviours and therefore sit far closer to the probe's decision
boundary than ordinary instructions do. The probe's $1\%$ operating point is thus an artefact
of the calibration corpus rather than a transferable property --- direct evidence for the
topical-separability concern raised above --- while the perplexity and token-anomaly filters
do not share the problem.
\textbf{Judging.} Breach is scored by a single automated rubric grader at the standard
binarisation threshold, without human adjudication on this corpus. Of the $1885$ responses
actually sent to the grader --- blocked inputs are never judged --- $66$, or $3.5\%$, did not
parse into the rubric format and are counted as non-breach, so the reported ASRs are very
slightly conservative.

\textbf{Judge reproducibility.} The grader is not deterministic, and the reported intervals do
not cover it. Re-judging an identical set of $300$ undefended responses with the same rubric,
model and temperature produced different verdicts on $19$ of them ($6.3\%$), only one of which
involved a parse failure; the undefended adaptive ASR moved from $0.58$ to $0.64$ on the
re-judge alone. The bootstrap intervals throughout this section resample behaviours and
therefore quantify sampling variation, not grader variation. Differences in ASR of a few
points should not be read as meaningful. Correlations are plausibly more robust than levels,
since a shifted verdict moves both members of a pair, but we have not measured that and do not
claim it.
\textbf{Stack composition.} The direct-stack measurement uses one fixed layer ordering; we
do not vary the order, and with Llama~Guard present the assembled residual is near zero,
